Interlining and garment shape: glue, hand and what you see on the face

  • Product Introduction

Posted by TrimGem On Jul 17 2025

The shell is the skin. Interlining is the skeleton. Fusing is not “sticking two cloths.” It is choosing how a front, a collar, a facing will stand, flex and look after a press and after a wash. Dots on a light wool, a PE that turns a placket into plywood, a PA that loves dry-clean but hates a 40 °C spin — those are shape decisions.

TrimGem treats coating and polymer as silhouette tools, not as mill trivia.

Three results that are not “peel”

Face. Light shirts, pale wools, fine facings: the wrong dot telegraphs, glazes, or casts a shadow. You see it more on a hanging garment than on a 10 cm swatch. Finish will not fully erase it.

Hand. A swatch card that feels soft can fuse into a board. Melted adhesive plus shell can lock a placket or a pocket mouth. On womenswear and shirts, hand is often the real QC, not the newton.

After wash / steam. Flat at the press is not flat after laundry. Edges lift, fronts bubble, hems ripple. That is the bond relaxing or shrinking against the shell — a coating and shrinkage problem, not a sewing problem.

If those three pass, the coating did its job. If peel is high but the face is ugly, you still failed.

Polymer is silhouette

  • PA (copolyamide). Soft, springy, dry-clean durable. Jacket and coat default when the care label is dry-clean. Wrong PA on a polyester shirt program: wash peel drops.
  • PES (copolyester). Wash-durable, friendly to polyester shells and imitation-silk polyesters. Fashion and washable tailoring.
  • PE / HDPE. Crisp, wash-hardy. Collars, cuffs, anything allowed to be a board. Not a draped front.

Wrong polymer cannot be ironed into the right hand.

Dot density and coat weight sit next to polymer. Heavy shells: fewer, larger dots, enough melt to wet a thick cloth. Light shells: more, finer dots, low gsm base, so the face does not read as a grid. “Soft and standing” on a thin base is a double-dot skill (see coating note), not a heavier scatter.

Press feeling is not process control

The fusing note covers windows. Here the shape reminder: a sample press is not a continuous fuser. Operators change. Coated rain shells act as release paper — the melt cannot key. Elastane shells shrink on heat; the interlining may not; cooling then bubbles. Those bubbles are strain, not dirt.

Fusible tailoring versus canvas

Industrial jackets fuse fronts, lapels, collars, cuffs, pockets. They ship. They can look a little dead. Adhesive can age; bubbling is the classic complaint.

Compared with canvas:

  • Fusible — fast, flatter, more rigid, bubble risk.
  • Half-canvas — chest spring with a fused lower front; still a fuse below.
  • Full canvas — hang, air, labour.

None of that makes fusibles “out of court.” It makes them a specified industrial tool. Choose PA, a double-dot, a sane window, and a wash/dry-clean remaining peel if the jacket is a fusible jacket. Do not sell a PE collar canvas as a Milanese front.

The check

  1. Face clean — no grid, no glaze, no shadow.
  2. Hand right for the zone (collar may be crisp; front should not).
  3. Still flat after the care you wrote on the label.

Those three are the shape spec. Peel is how you prove the skeleton will still be there next season.
