Hook-and-loop chemistry, recycled content and flame ratings

  • Buyer Guides

发布 TrimGem 于 2025年2月6日

Hook-and-loop sits on skin, in cars, on kids’ shoes and next to electronics. The textile story (hook, loop, peel) is not the compliance story. Dye, finish, adhesive and fibre all carry restricted substances. Flame is a separate claim. Recycled content is a third. Mixing them into “eco FR magic tape” is how a shipment fails the wrong test.

TrimGem puts certificates on the article and the colourway, not on the mill’s front door.

Restricted substances

Apparel and infant programs typically ask for:

  • OEKO-TEX® STANDARD 100 — Product Class I if the tape is for babies; Class II if it is next to skin. Covers a long list of restricted chemistries on the textile.
  • REACH — including SVHC screening and Appendix XVII restrictions that bite dyes and some plasticisers.
  • EN 71-3 — when the article is a toy or is sold as one; migration of certain metals.

Practical lab lines that still appear on Chinese export packs: formaldehyde (ISO 14184-1), banned azo colourants (ISO 14362-1), wash fastness (ISO 105-C06). Infant goods care about saliva and a tighter formaldehyde cap. None of this is optional because the tape is “only a cuff.”

Adhesive-backed tapes add the glue to the RSL. Silicone skin adhesives and solvent acrylics are different chemistries. Test the SKU you will sew or stick.

Flame

Two routes:

  1. Inherent. FR fibres (including some aramids) with a high LOI (trade talk: >28), char rather than melt-drip. Used on protective clothing tapes.
  2. Treated. FR chemistry in the polymer or as a finish. Finishes can wash down. Name the wash claim.

Customer methods you will be handed: UL 94 V-0 (vertical, self-extinguishing, a plastics language often borrowed onto tapes), NFPA 701, garment FR standards that include the trim. Anti-static tapes (surface resistance in a 10⁶–10⁹ Ω band, carbon or anti-stat finish) are an electronics/cleanroom article, not a flame article.

A public-building curtain tape and a kids’ shoe strap do not share a flame rating. If the garment is not FR, do not pay for FR hook-and-loop — and do not print an FR icon on the hangtag.

Recycled and bio-based

GRS (Global Recycled Standard) is the chain-of-custody language for recycled content. Recycled polyamide from pre-consumer or post-consumer streams is spun, woven and dyed like virgin if the mill is serious. Performance can match a virgin nylon hook-and-loop: high cycle life (published examples at 10 000 opens), wash and dry-clean, AZO-free dye, OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 Class I plus GRS on the same SKU.

That combination is the existence proof. It is not automatic on every “RPET tape” quote. Ask for the transaction certificate and the test report on that width and colour.

PLA and other bio-based experiments exist for compostable stories. They are not a drop-in nylon hook. Treat them as a development article with a use-temperature and a hydrolysis conversation.

Recycling a tape that was glued to foam is a different problem from buying GRS yarn. Do not claim circularity from a GRS logo on a mixed-material composite.

What to file

For EU apparel: OEKO-TEX or an equivalent RSL pack (formaldehyde, azo, APEO, extractable metals, phthalates as applicable), plus REACH statements.

For FR workwear: the garment standard’s trim clause and the tape’s burn method.

For recycled claims: GRS scope and percentage, fibre origin (pre- vs post-consumer), and a peel/shear report so the green tape still closes.

For toys and infant: EN 71-3 / Class I, moulded hook, attachment strength to the garment.

Write article + colour + certificate + expiry. A mill certificate from 2019 on a different width is not a file. TrimGem’s quotes name the cert on the line so a cuff tape is as documentable as a snap.
