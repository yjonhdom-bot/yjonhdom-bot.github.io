Lining fibres and hand: polyester, acetate, cupro, viscose

  • Product Introduction

Posted by TrimGem On Nov 13 2025

Lining is the inner cloth between the wearer and the rest of the garment. It is not interlining (the skeleton that shapes a collar or a front). It is not wadding (the lofted fill). Lining slides you into a jacket, hides seams and canvas, cuts cling, and can add a thin air layer. Summer linings are there to be light and to move moisture. Winter linings are there to be kind on wool and to let a coat on and off.

TrimGem samples lining qualities next to the shell program: fibre, construction, gsm. A “red lining” is a colour, not a SKU.

Cupro (Bemberg-type)

Cuprammonium rayon from cotton linters. Tailors still treat good cupro as the suit-lining reference: breathable, inherently low-static compared with polyester, fine drape, more durable than commodity viscose or acetate, biodegradable as a cellulose. Typical suit weights 80–110 g/m² (often ~100), yarns in the 30–75 denier band, plain, twill or satin. Asahi Kasei’s Bemberg is the famous industrial story; the generic is cupro. Use: men’s jackets, better women’s coats and dresses.

Viscose (rayon)

Regenerated cellulose, soft, breathable, mid-price, good drape, weaker wet, more shrink and crease than cupro. Often blended with polyester or cupro. Typical 80–120 g/m². Mid tailoring and dresses.

Acetate

Silk-like at a price below silk and cupro. Light (60–90 g/m²), decent drape, less durable than polyester, water-spots, usually dry-clean. Mid jackets.

Silk

Luxury: slide, lustre, climate, drape. 60–80 g/m² typical. Not a workhorse. Water-spots, careful clean, evening and ceremonial.

Polyester

The volume lining. Durable, crease-resistant, cheap. Plain taffeta / polyester pongee in about 50–70 g/m² is the default jacket and down-proof inner. Slippery, a little bright, poor moisture, static unless anti-stat finished. Washable. Honest on a supermarket coat; stingy on a canvassed suit unless the hand is upgraded (peach, dull yarn, better drape).

Cotton

Matte, breathable, kind on skin, 100–160 g/m². Casual and summer jackets. Less slide, more crease, can grab. Not a tuxedo lining.

Natural (cotton, silk), regenerated (viscose, acetate, cupro), and synthetics (polyester, polyamide) plus blends (including traditional viscose/filament “beautiful silk” types) all appear. Polyester and nylon slide and static. Viscose and acetate shrink and lose wet strength — pattern must allow it. Cotton is kind and dull.

Spec line

Write fibre, gsm, weave, dull or bright, anti-stat, care. Match lining lighter and more flexible than the shell (fur and pile excepted). Do not call a fusible a lining. Do not call a 120 g wadding a lining. The next note is density (T) and tear. This note is what the wearer feels.
